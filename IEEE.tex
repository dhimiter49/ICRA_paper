\begin{abstract}
    Model Predictive Control (MPC) has been successful in dealing with complex and safety critical systems. The development of fast quadratic programming solvers has allowed for MPC to be implemented in robotics where real-time interaction is of the essence. The effectiveness of MPC is the ability to plan its behaviour while adhering to arbitrary constraints. To do so MPC needs to model the environment and the task and run its optimization for a limited horizon. Classically, the horizon limits both the planning (which usually comes in the form of the quadratic objective) and the safety (usually in the form of linear constraints). We propose a new MPC scheme, cascading MPC (CMPC) where planning is decoupled from safety and run experiments in order to offer insight and analysis of the trade-off between long, costly but safe horizon planning, to quick, efficient and potentially sufficient short horizon planning. We present results and compare to the nominal MPC framework on the navigation task. It is shown that while planning does not benefit from a long horizon, safety gradually improves until a certain horizon length. The novel approach also puts forward a new way of dealing with comparative benchmarking.
\end{abstract}

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}\label{sec:problem}

The navigation problem can be mathematically formulated as a control problem with state-space noted with $\mathcal{X}$, control-space noted with $\mathcal{U}$ and cost function $\mathcal{C}$. We assume deterministic transitions probabilities (dynamics) $\mathcal{P}(x,u)$ without introducing any noise. Generally, the navigation task involves a robot moving through a crowd (agents implemented with some other policy, e.g. constant velocity) with a given goal position. The optimization problem at time step $t$ for MPC can be written as
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:mpc}
    \min \;\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits^N_{k=0}\mathcal{C}(x_{t+k}, u_{t+k})\right],\\
    \text{s.t.} \quad s_{t+k+1} = f(x_{t+k}, u_{t+k}),\nonumber
\end{align}
The horizon $N$ is set preemptively and constraints are respected during its entirely. The model $f$ used in MPC is usually a conservative (in terms of safety) approximation of $\mathcal{P}$. MPC optimizes control based on its model, however the model is imperfect which is the reason why a feedback-control is used for each state and optimized for the horizon.

\subsection{MPC for Navigation of a Holonomic Robot}

We assume an holonomic robot which implies straight forward dynamics from classical mechanics for the given agent position $\boldsymbol{p}_k$ at time $k$
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:dynamics}
    \boldsymbol{p}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{p}_k + \dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_kT + \dfrac{1}{2}T^2\ddot{\boldsymbol{p}}_k &= \boldsymbol{p}_k + \dfrac{1}{2}\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_kT + \dfrac{1}{2}T\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_{k+1}\\
    \text{and} \quad \dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_{k+1} = \dot{\boldsymbol{p}}+T\ddot{\boldsymbol{p}} \;& \Leftrightarrow \; \ddot{\boldsymbol{p}}=\dfrac{\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_{k+1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_k}{T}.\nonumber
\end{align}
To simplify the problem and reduce the numerical complexity (especially important when implementing cascading MPC), we apply velocity control. We further omit the step indexes when possible to avoid clutter. Iteratively we derive the transition matrix for a given horizon $N$ and current step $\boldsymbol{p}_0$ based on \Cref{eq:dynamics}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:dynamics_horizon}
    &\quad\quad\quad\quad P=P_0+\dfrac{1}{2}T\dot{P}_0+M_{pv}\dot{P}, \quad (P=\boldsymbol{p}_{1:N}),\nonumber\\
    &P=\begin{bmatrix}P^{x\intercal} & P^{y\intercal}\end{bmatrix}^\intercal, \;\text{where} \;P^x=p^x_0+\dfrac{1}{2}T\dot{p}^x_0+M_{xv}\dot{P}^x,\nonumber\\
        &M_{xv} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2} & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 1 & \frac{1}{2} & \ddots & \vdots \\ \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0 \\ 1 & \dots & 1 & \frac{1}{2} \end{bmatrix}T, M_{pv}=\begin{bmatrix}
            M_{xv} & \boldsymbol{0} \\
            \boldsymbol{0} & M_{yv} \\
        \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
where $P^x$ and $P^y$ are computed equivalently and $M_{xv}=M_{yv}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$. In the following, $x$ and $y$ will not be treated separately and we will concatenate appropriately to compute $P_0=\begin{bmatrix}
    p^x_0, & \dots & p^x_0, & p^y_0, & \dots & p^y_0
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$, $\dot{P}_0\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$, $M_{pv}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}$ and $\dot{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$.

Given our model we can define our cost function as the distance to a reference trajectory, $\frac{1}{2}\vert\vert P - P^{ref}\vert\vert$ (e.g. $P^{ref}$ is a straight line to the goal). This gives us a quadratic function to optimize after applying \Cref{eq:dynamics_horizon}
\begin{align*}
    &\min\limits_{\dot{P}}\frac{1}{2}\vert\vert P - P^{ref}\vert\vert \Leftrightarrow \min\limits_{\dot{P}}\frac{1}{2}\left(P-P^{ref}\right)^\intercal\left(P-P^{ref}\right) \nonumber\\
    \Leftrightarrow&\min\limits_{\dot{P}} \frac{1}{2}\dot{P}^\intercal\underbrace{M_{pv}^\intercal M_{pv}}_{\substack{=Q \\ \text{quadratic} \\ \text{term}}}\dot{P} +  \underbrace{\left(P_0-P^{ref}+ \frac{1}{2}T\dot{P}_0\right)^\intercal M_{pv}}_{=q \;\text{linear term}}\dot{P}.\nonumber
\end{align*}
Linear constraints can be arbitrarily added to this objective, see \Cref{tab:constraints}. For the crowd distance constraints we assume that the members of the crowd maintain the current velocity and increase the safety distance by the maximum distance in the error of the prediction. The maximum error will be lower than the distance covered during time-step $T$ with $\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_{max}$, as represented in \Cref{fig:uncert}.

\section{METHOD}

    In an attempt to decouple efficiency from safety we propose to implement a cascading stream of braking trajectories instead of ensuring safety simply from the current start position of the agent. This implies two horizons, $M$ for planning and $N$ for safety. The equation representing this objective is
    \begin{equation}
        \min\limits_{\dot{P}}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\vert\left\vert P - P^{ref}\right\vert\right\vert \; \text{and} \; \boldsymbol{p}_i = \boldsymbol{b}_{i,1}, \boldsymbol{\dot{p}}_i = \boldsymbol{\dot{b}}_{i,1}\; \forall i\in \{1,\dots,M\},
        \label{eq:cascade_traj}
    \end{equation}
    where $P$ and $\dot{P}$ are defined the same as previously whereas $B$, $\dot{B}$ and elements $b_j^i$ will represent the full cascading plan that includes multiple trajectories. In this notation $i$ represents the planning step between 1 and $M$ whereas $j$ the braking step between 1 and $N$. The \Cref{eq:cascade_traj} indicates that the steps in the planning horizon $P$ are identical to the first steps of all braking trajectories. \Cref{fig:cascading} represents four braking trajectories indexed with $i=\{1,2,3,M\}$ while the plan is represented by the black line going from the agent in green to the green cross representing the goal.

    \subsection{Change in Dynamics}
        The full control computed is linearized as
        \begin{align*}
            \dot{B}^x:=\Bigl[&\dot{b}^x_{1,1},  \dots, \dot{b}^x_{1,N-1}, \dot{b}^x_{2,1}, \dots, \dot{b}^x_{2,N-1}, \dots\\
             &\dot{b}^x_{M,1}, \dots, \dot{b}^x_{M,N-1} \Bigr]^\intercal \in \mathbb{R}^{M(N-1)},
        \end{align*}
        where safety trajectories go up to $N-1$ as we specify again explicitly that the last control value for each braking trajectory is zero, $\dot{\boldsymbol{b}}_{i,N}=0$, $\forall i \in \{1,\dots M\}$. For 2D we generalize $B=\begin{bmatrix}B_x, & B_y \end{bmatrix}^\intercal$ where $B_y$ is defined analogously to $B_x$\.

            Inside braking trajectories the dynamics remain unchanged (we still use $M_{xv}$ in the diagonal of $M_{b\dot{b}x}$), however the initial step $b_{i,1}$ will be relative to all other previous steps $b_{k,1}$ where $k<i$. This correlation is ensured with matrix $F$ (in the lower triangle of $M_{b\dot{b}x}$), similar to the lower triangle in \Cref{eq:dynamics_horizon}. The auxiliary vectors $B_0,\dot{B}_0\in\mathcal{R}^{M(N-1)}$ are defined the same as $P_0$ and $\dot{P}_0$, in order to write the new matrix form of the transition dynamics
        \begin{align*}
            B&=B_0+\dfrac{1}{2}T\dot{B}_0+ M_{b\dot{b}}\dot{B},
            % \text{where} \quad B_0=\begin{bmatrix} bx_0 \\ \vdots \\ bx_0 \\ by_0 \\ \vdots \\ by_0 \end{bmatrix},\dot{B}_0=\begin{bmatrix} \dot{bx}_0 \\ \vdots \\ \dot{bx}_0 \\ \dot{by}_0 \\ \vdots \\ \dot{by}_0 \end{bmatrix}
            \\
            M_{b\dot{b}} &= \begin{bmatrix} M_{b\dot{b}x} & 0 \\ 0 & M_{b\dot{b}y}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2MN\times 2M(N - 1)},\\
                M_{b\dot{b}x} &=
            \begin{bmatrix}
                M_{xv} & 0 & \dots & 0\\
                F & M_{xv} & \ddots & \vdots\\
                \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0\\
                F & \dots & F & M_{xv} \\
            \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{MN\times M(N - 1)},\\
                F&=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \end{bmatrix}T\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N-1}\\
                & \quad M_{xv} = M_{yv}, \quad M_{b\dot{b}x}=M_{b\dot{b}y}.
        \end{align*}

    \subsection{Objective}
         For the objective we need to follow the reference trajectory $P^{ref}$ which relate to the first index of each braking trajectory $b_1^i\; \forall i\in\{1,\dots M\}$. We accomplish this by filtering the irrelevant indexes in $B$
         \begin{align*}
             F_P = \begin{bmatrix}
                 F_X & 0 \\ 0 & F_Y
             \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{where} \quad &F_X=\begin{bmatrix}
                 f & 0 & \dots & 0\\
                 0 & f & \ddots & \vdots\\
                 \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0\\
                 0 & \dots & 0 & f\\
                 \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M(N-1)},
                 \\ \quad &F_X=F_Y, \quad f=\begin{bmatrix} 1, & 0, & \dots & 0 \end{bmatrix}\in \mathbb{R}^{1\times (N - 1)},
         \end{align*}
         in this way the objective function can be written as
         \begin{equation*}
              \dfrac{1}{2}\min\limits_{\dot{B}}\vert\vert F_PB - P^{ref}\vert\vert\Leftrightarrow \dfrac{1}{2}\min\limits_{\dot{P}}\vert\vert P-P^{ref}\vert\vert.
         \end{equation*}
         The resulting QP is equivalent to the simple MPC formulation
         \begin{align*}
             Q &= (F_PM_{b\dot{b}})^\intercal(F_PM_{b\dot{b}})=M_{pv}^\intercal M_{pv}\\
             v &= \left(P_0 - P^{ref} + \dfrac{1}{2}\dot{P}_0\right)^\intercal F_PM_{b\dot{b}}=\left(P_0 - P^{ref} + \dfrac{1}{2}\dot{P}_0\right)^\intercal M_{pv}.
         \end{align*}

    \subsection{Constraints}

        \subsubsection{Obstacle Avoidance}
            Collision avoidance constraints do not change at all as they are applied equally for each step. In this case however the crowd position must to be predicted for $M+N$ steps. The crowd position horizon vector for member $k$ would look like the following in order to relate to the new control vector $B$
            \begin{align*}
                M_k=[&\begin{matrix} m_{k,1}^x, & \dots & m_{k,N}^x, & m_{k,2}^x, & \dots & m_{k,N+1}^x, & \dots & m_{k,M-N}^x, & \dots & m_{k,M+N}^x,\end{matrix}\\
                &\begin{matrix} m_{k,1}^y, & \dots & m_{k,N}^y, & m_{k,2}^y, & \dots & m_{k,N+1}^y, & \dots & m_{k,M-N}^y, & \dots & m_{k,M+N}^y \end{matrix}]^\intercal
            \end{align*}

        \subsubsection{Limiting Velocity and Acceleration}
            Velocity is limited the same as the original MPC \Cref{eq:vel_const} because it is applied uniformly over the entire trajectory $B$, whereas acceleration needs a more careful consideration. The constraint introduced in \Cref{eq:acc_const} needs to be updated in order to consider the dependency between the first step of each braking trajectory while keeping the dependency inside the braking trajectories. The resulting matrix resembles the structure of $M_{b\dot{b}}$ introduced previously
            \begin{align*}
                & M_{al}\begin{bmatrix} D_B & 0 \\ 0 & D_B\end{bmatrix}\dot{B}<\hat{b}_{al}+\dfrac{M_{al}}{T}\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_0, & 0, & \dots & \dot{y}_0, & 0, & \dots \end{bmatrix}^\intercal \\
                \text{where} \quad & D_B = \begin{bmatrix}
                    D & 0 & \dots & \dots & 0 \\
                    F_D & D & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\
                    0 & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\
                    \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & D & 0 \\
                    0 & \dots & 0 & F_D & D
                \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{MN\times M(N - 1)} \quad \text{and} \quad F_D=\begin{bmatrix}
                    -1 & 0 & \dots & 0\\
                    0 & 0 & \ddots & \vdots\\
                    \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0\\
                    0 & \dots & 0 & 0
                \end{bmatrix}, \nonumber
            \end{align*}
            with appropriate dimension for $M_{al}$ and $b_{al}$ in order to comply with the dimensionality of $\dot{B}$.


    \subsection{Problem Complexity \& Dimensionality}
        Using the same specification as in \Cref{eq:env_specs} and the same analysis, we can compute the number of constraints for a planning horizon of 5 and safety horizon 21. The number of wall constraints would be $4\times 21 \times 5$. $8 \times 21 \times 5$ and $8 \times 20 \times 5$ for acceleration and velocity constraints respectively. Finally, for moving members of the crowd there are $6 \times 21 \times 5$ constraints bringing the total to 2690. After filtering out irrelevant constraints during execution on average the QP-solver takes into consideration 1570 constraints.
